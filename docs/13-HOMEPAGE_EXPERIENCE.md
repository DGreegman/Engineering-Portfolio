# 13 — Homepage Experience

## Purpose

The homepage is not a marketing landing page.

It is the front door to an engineering workspace.

Rather than persuading visitors with claims, the homepage should earn their curiosity by demonstrating thoughtful engineering, clear communication, and a genuine passion for learning.

The objective is to leave visitors thinking:

> "I want to see how this engineer approaches problems."

If that feeling exists after the first minute, the homepage has succeeded.

---

# Experience Philosophy

The homepage should feel like walking into the workspace of an engineer who has momentarily stepped away.

Everything should communicate calmness, clarity, and craftsmanship.

The interface should never compete with the content.

Instead, it should quietly support exploration.

The visitor should feel invited rather than marketed to.

---

# Core Principles

## Curiosity Before Conversion

The homepage exists to create curiosity.

It should never feel like it is trying to sell the engineer.

Every section should naturally encourage the visitor to continue exploring.

---

## Knowledge Before Identity

Engineering thinking should be introduced before personal biography.

Trust should emerge from demonstrated knowledge rather than personal claims.

The engineer is introduced after the visitor has already experienced the quality of the work.

---

## Documentation First

The homepage should resemble the beginning of a well-maintained engineering repository.

Its visual language is inspired by documentation rather than marketing.

Whitespace, typography, and structure should take precedence over decoration.

---

## Calm by Default

The interface should remain intentionally quiet.

Avoid:

- excessive motion
- large illustrations
- oversized hero sections
- decorative gradients
- unnecessary visual effects

Every design decision should reduce cognitive load.

---

## Authentic Engineering

Engineering references should feel natural.

Small details such as README styling, terminal snippets, repository language, and workspace metaphors should reinforce the experience without becoming gimmicks.

The goal is subtle personality, not novelty.

---

## Progressive Discovery

The homepage should answer one question at a time.

Every section should satisfy one curiosity while creating the next.

The experience should unfold naturally as the visitor scrolls.

---

# Visitor Journey

The homepage should guide visitors through six questions.

## 1. Where am I?

The visitor immediately understands:

This is an engineering knowledge platform.

Not a traditional portfolio.

Not a personal website.

---

## 2. Why should I stay?

The visitor recognizes thoughtful engineering and clear communication.

Curiosity begins.

---

## 3. How does this engineer think?

Engineering principles become visible.

Visitors begin understanding the mindset behind the work.

---

## 4. Can I trust this engineer?

Knowledge, architecture, and engineering decisions provide evidence.

Trust is earned through substance rather than claims.

---

## 5. What should I explore next?

The homepage introduces knowledge, work, and engineering logs.

Navigation becomes exploration rather than browsing.

---

## 6. Who is behind this?

Only after trust has been established does the homepage introduce the engineer.

Identity reinforces the experience rather than leading it.

---

# Homepage Narrative

The homepage tells one continuous story.

```
Welcome.

↓

Discover the workspace.

↓

Understand the mindset.

↓

See the knowledge.

↓

Explore the engineering work.

↓

Meet the engineer.

↓

Continue learning.
```

Every section should naturally lead into the next.

No section should feel isolated.

---

# Homepage Structure

## Workspace Header

Persistent navigation.

Provides orientation without competing for attention.

---

## README Introduction

Purpose:

Introduce the Engineering Workspace.

The first screen should resemble the README of an engineering repository.

This section establishes tone rather than providing biography.

Possible supporting elements:

- README heading
- short engineering statement
- subtle terminal snippet
- primary action leading to knowledge

---

## Current Focus

Purpose:

Show what the engineer is actively exploring.

Rather than listing skills, highlight ongoing learning.

This reinforces curiosity and continuous growth.

---

## How I Think

Purpose:

Introduce engineering philosophy.

Rather than explaining experience, communicate principles.

Examples include:

- Simplicity over complexity.
- Reliability before cleverness.
- Documentation is part of engineering.
- Security is a feature.
- Learn publicly.

These are principles, not marketing statements.

---

## Knowledge Preview

Purpose:

Demonstrate engineering depth.

Display a curated selection of recent knowledge.

The objective is to encourage exploration.

---

## Featured Work

Purpose:

Show engineering stories rather than project galleries.

Each item should communicate:

- problem
- approach
- trade-offs
- outcome

Technology should support the story rather than define it.

---

## Engineering Log

Purpose:

Show continuous learning.

Unlike polished knowledge articles, Engineering Logs capture exploration, experiments, and discoveries.

This section reinforces authenticity.

---

## Meet the Engineer

Purpose:

Introduce the person behind the workspace.

Include:

- concise biography
- current interests
- professional links
- portrait
- contact

The emphasis remains on engineering rather than personal branding.

---

## Workspace Footer

The footer concludes the reading experience quietly.

It should encourage continued exploration without becoming promotional.

---

# Visual Philosophy

The homepage should feel:

- timeless
- minimal
- calm
- spacious
- readable
- trustworthy

Typography should carry the visual hierarchy.

Whitespace should separate ideas naturally.

Every element should feel intentional.

---

# Interaction Principles

Interactions should feel immediate and unobtrusive.

Animations should support orientation rather than decoration.

Hover states, focus states, and transitions should reinforce clarity.

Nothing should move without a reason.

Keyboard users should experience the same quality as pointer users.

---

# Engineering Workspace Identity

The Engineering Workspace is more than a visual style.

It is a product philosophy.

The workspace represents:

- thoughtful engineering
- continuous learning
- clear documentation
- deliberate design
- craftsmanship

Every component should reinforce these values.

---

# Success Criteria

The homepage succeeds if visitors leave with the following impressions:

- This engineer thinks deeply.
- This engineer documents their work well.
- This engineer enjoys learning.
- I trust the quality of their engineering.
- I want to continue exploring.

Failure occurs if visitors primarily remember:

- the animations
- the visuals
- the technology stack
- the framework

The knowledge and engineering mindset should always remain the focus.

---

# Things to Avoid

Do not:

- build a traditional portfolio hero
- lead with a résumé
- place a large portrait above the fold
- showcase technology logos as the primary message
- prioritize personal branding over engineering thinking
- overwhelm visitors with information
- create unnecessary visual complexity

The homepage should feel like an invitation into an engineer's workspace—not a presentation about the engineer.

---

# Final Principle

The homepage should never attempt to impress.

It should quietly invite curiosity.

If visitors finish the first minute wanting to read one more article, explore one more case study, or understand one more engineering decision, then the homepage has achieved its purpose.
