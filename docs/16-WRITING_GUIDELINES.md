# 16 — Writing Guidelines

## Purpose

This document defines the writing standards for the Engineering Workspace.

Its purpose is to ensure every article, engineering log, and case study shares a consistent structure, tone, and level of quality.

Writing should prioritize clarity, engineering judgment, and long-term usefulness over publishing frequency.

The goal is to create an engineering library rather than a collection of blog posts.

---

# Writing Philosophy

Every article should help readers think more clearly.

Writing is not about demonstrating expertise.

It is about reducing confusion.

Readers should leave with better engineering judgment rather than simply more information.

Whenever possible:

Explain why.

Discuss trade-offs.

Provide context.

Encourage deeper thinking.

---

# Tone of Voice

The Engineering Workspace should sound:

Calm

Thoughtful

Curious

Honest

Technical

Approachable

Avoid:

Marketing language

Clickbait

Exaggeration

Buzzwords

Unnecessary hype

Write like an experienced engineer explaining an idea to another engineer.

---

# The Reader

Assume readers are:

Software engineers

Computer science students

Backend engineers

Self-taught developers

Curious learners

Never assume complete beginners.

Never assume experts.

Explain enough that motivated engineers can follow the discussion.

---

# Standard Article Structure

Every knowledge article should follow the same overall structure.

1. Introduction

Explain the problem.

Why does it matter?

When does it appear?

---

2. The Concept

Define the concept clearly.

Avoid unnecessary jargon.

Use precise terminology.

---

3. Why It Matters

Explain practical implications.

Discuss real-world scenarios.

---

4. Implementation

Introduce code only after readers understand the idea.

Prefer small focused examples.

---

5. Trade-offs

Every engineering decision has costs.

Discuss:

Advantages

Disadvantages

Limitations

Alternatives

When not to use it

---

6. Common Mistakes

Highlight misconceptions.

Explain why they occur.

Show the correct approach.

---

7. Key Takeaways

Summarize the important ideas.

Readers should be able to scan this section later.

---

8. Related Learning

Suggest:

Prerequisites

Related Topics

Series

Next Reading

---

# Engineering Principles

Articles should emphasize:

Trade-offs

Architecture

Maintainability

Reliability

Performance

Security

Scalability

Observability

Engineering is rarely about perfect solutions.

It is about choosing the most appropriate solution.

---

# Code Examples

Code exists to explain ideas.

Not to impress readers.

Guidelines:

Keep examples focused.

Avoid unnecessary boilerplate.

Explain why code exists.

Prefer production-inspired examples.

Include comments only when they improve understanding.

---

# Diagrams

Use diagrams whenever they improve understanding.

Suitable diagrams include:

Architecture

Sequence

Flow

State transitions

Request lifecycle

Avoid diagrams that simply decorate the page.

---

# Callouts

Use callouts intentionally.

Available types:

Note

Tip

Warning

Security

Performance

Trade-off

Architecture

Avoid excessive callouts.

---

# Titles

Titles should answer questions.

Prefer:

Why UUIDs Are Not Always the Best Primary Key

How Rate Limiting Protects APIs

Understanding the Node.js Event Loop

Avoid vague titles.

---

# Introductions

The first paragraph should answer:

Why should I care?

Avoid long personal stories.

Get to the engineering problem quickly.

---

# Conclusions

Do not simply repeat the introduction.

Instead:

Summarize the lesson.

Reinforce the engineering judgment.

Encourage deeper exploration.

---

# Case Studies

Case studies should tell stories.

Structure:

Problem

Constraints

Architecture

Implementation

Trade-offs

Outcome

Lessons Learned

Focus on decisions rather than features.

---

# Engineering Logs

Engineering Logs are intentionally lightweight.

Capture:

Experiments

Observations

Failures

Ideas

Lessons

Questions

They are snapshots of ongoing learning.

Not polished articles.

---

# Internal Linking

Every article should connect to other knowledge.

Prefer meaningful links over excessive linking.

Readers should naturally discover related concepts.

---

# Evergreen Content

Articles should remain valuable for years.

Avoid unnecessary references to temporary trends unless discussing them is the article's purpose.

Focus on engineering concepts rather than specific framework versions whenever possible.

---

# Quality Checklist

Before publishing, ask:

Does the introduction explain why this matters?

Does the article teach engineering judgment?

Have trade-offs been discussed?

Are examples focused?

Does every code sample have a purpose?

Are related topics linked?

Would this article still be useful two years from now?

If any answer is "No", continue refining before publishing.

---

# Success Metric

A successful article does not make readers think:

"I learned another programming trick."

A successful article makes readers think:

"I understand why this engineering decision matters."
